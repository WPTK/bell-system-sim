NAME
     bell_system_unix - Bell System UNIX Version 7 Internal Operations Terminal Simulation

SYNOPSIS
     bell-system [--tutorial] [--role N] [--simple] [--version]
     python -m bell_system [--tutorial] [--role N] [--simple] [--version]

DESCRIPTION
     The Bell System UNIX V7 Terminal Simulation provides an authentic recreation 
     of AT&T Bell System internal operations workstations from 1978-1983. This 
     historically accurate simulation reproduces the experience of using original 
     UNIX V7 systems with period-accurate Bell System commands, workflows, trouble 
     tickets, and role-specific operations.

     The simulation is based on extensive Bell System Technical Journal 
     documentation, Engineering and Operations manuals, Bell System Practices 
     (BSP), and authentic AT&T internal procedures from the pre-divestiture era.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT
     This simulation represents Bell System operations during the critical period 
     of 1978-1983, when the company was transitioning from electromechanical to 
     electronic switching systems while preparing for the 1984 divestiture. The 
     simulation includes authentic equipment (3A Central Control, 5ESS, TH-3 
     microwave, TNDS), procedures, and terminology from this transformative period 
     in telecommunications history.

INSTALLATION
     Requirements:
       - Python 3.9 or higher
       - Terminal emulator supporting ANSI escape sequences
       - Unix-like operating system (Linux, macOS, BSD) or Windows with WSL

     Setup:
       1. Ensure Python 3 is installed: python3 --version
       2. Clone the repository and install it: pip install -e .
       3. Run the simulation: bell-system

USAGE
     Start the simulation with the bell-system command. You will be presented
     with a role selection menu offering 12 authentic Bell System positions.
     After selecting a role, you'll receive a shift briefing and access to
     role-appropriate commands. The --role option takes a number from 1 to 12
     and skips the menu.

     Commands are entered at the Unix-style prompt. Type 'help' for available 
     commands or 'man <command>' for detailed documentation.

     To exit the simulation, use Ctrl+C or the 'exit' command.

THE REPAIR SERVICE BUREAU
     A customer trouble report arrives on your board with nothing on it but
     the customer's own words. What is actually on the pair is not known
     until it is measured. Engineering and Operations in the Bell System
     gives the corrective maintenance sequence as detect, notify, verify,
     locate, repair, verify, and says plainly that locating the trouble is
     the most difficult and time consuming step. That is the shape of this
     work.

     report
         The pending list, nearest commitment first

     report show <n>
         The line record, what the customer said, and what has been done

     report callback <n>
         Telephone the customer for more than the card carries

     report dispatch <n> <force>
         Send a repair force. The wrong force costs forty-five minutes and
         the trouble is still there

     report close <n> 5 <fault>
         Trouble found. Name the condition you found and cleared

     report close <n> 8
         No trouble found. Closed without a repair

     Codes 5 and 8 are published Bell System dispositions, counted
     separately in the network switching performance measurement plan.
     Closing a faulty line as code 8 does not fail loudly. It closes, and
     then the customer calls back - and that repeat is what the plan was
     counting.

     A report answers to its number (TR-04471), to the telephone number on
     it, or to its position on the board.

MECHANISED LOOP TESTING
     mlt <report or telephone number>

     Two different measurements are reported and they must not be confused.
     Insulation resistance is taken with the loop open and the office
     battery removed; it is high on a healthy pair and low wherever a fault
     is bridging something. Which of tip-ring, tip-ground and ring-ground
     is low is what tells you what the fault is. Loop resistance is the
     resistance of the pair itself with the loop closed, and it is the
     number the 1300-ohm design limit applies to.

     Capacitance is the third reading and the most useful one. Local
     exchange cable runs 0.083 microfarads to the mile, so a capacitance
     measurement on an open pair is a distance to the break.

     Infinite tip to ring, no station termination      open
     Near zero tip to ring                             short
     One conductor low to ground, tip-ring normal      ground
     Foreign potential with no battery applied         foreign EMF
     Several pairs low in one cable                    wet cable
     Within limits, loop closed, current flowing       receiver off hook
     Clean to the frame, customer still cut off        office equipment

TRANSMISSION TESTING
     testline [code] [circuit]

     The Bell System Technical Journal for April 1982 names the far-end
     test line series: the 102-type for a one-way loss measurement, the
     100-type for one-way loss and noise, and the 105-type responder for
     two-way loss, noise, noise with tone and gain slope. The balance test
     line connects the balance termination so office balance can be
     measured, and the remote office test line seizes a trunk from the far
     office under command and connects it to a 52A responder.

     Every loss reading is taken at 1004 Hz, because that is the frequency
     the Bell System stated its loss objectives at.

     CAROT - Centralized Automatic Reporting On Trunks - drives the remote
     office test line with multifrequency signalling and routines trunk
     groups without anybody asking it to. Its exceptions print to the
     maintenance teletype.

     testboard supervision <trunk group> reads what single frequency
     signalling says about a trunk. The 2600 Hz tone is on an idle trunk
     and removed when the trunk is seized, which makes the tone a
     supervisory signal a craftsperson reads directly. Tone present while a
     connection is up is an irregularity; report it and hold the circuit
     out of service.

TEST CALLS
     testcall <from> <to> [test line]

     A test call is how a trunk is proved. The stages are the real ones:

     Seizure          2600 Hz sits on an idle trunk. Seizing it removes the
                      tone toward the far end, which is what the far end
                      watches for.
     Start signal     Wink start winks off-hook and back; delay dial holds
                      off-hook until its register frees; immediate start
                      relies on a fixed interval instead.
     Address          Outpulsed in multifrequency, bracketed by KP to open
                      the register and ST to release it. The talking path is
                      muted while an office outpulses, which is why MF needs
                      no protection against the human voice.
     Route advance    High-usage group first, overflowing up the homing
                      chain to a final group. All trunks busy in a final
                      group means reorder.
     Answer           Answer supervision returns, tone off both directions.
     Release          Tone restored, trunk back to idle.

     Name a test line as the third argument and the connection is measured
     rather than merely completed. Loss accumulates on every trunk in
     tandem.

     Test numbers, test line access codes and start arrangements were all
     office records rather than national rules, so those here are the
     simulation's own.

DIFFICULTY
     set game.difficulty fun
         Fun Simulation. Loop testing names the fault it reads, a report
         may be closed without measuring, a wrong call costs little,
         commitments are not counted, and qualification comes quickly.

     set game.difficulty craft
         I Hate Myself. Loop testing prints the numbers and nothing else. A
         report cannot be closed until it has been measured. Wrongly closed
         lines come back as repeats. Missed commitments count.
         Qualification is four times slower and the building interrupts you
         four times as often.

     set game.ambience off
         Silence the other craft.

QUALIFICATION AND THE SERVICE INDEX
     What a craftsperson was allowed to work on was governed by
     qualification. You start signed off on Loop and Station, plus whatever
     your assigned position carries, and earn the rest a correctly closed
     report at a time.

     Loop and Station              report, mlt, trouble, testboard,
                                   testline
     Main Distributing Frame       cosmos, lmos
     Central Office Switching      switch, alarm, crossbar, 3a
     Switching Control Center      sarts, orderwire
     Interoffice Trunks            trunk, routing, dialtone
     Toll Network                  toll, tnds, traffic

     qual          The craft record
     qual index    The measurement weights the index is scored against

     The service index is scored against the weights published in the
     network switching performance measurement plan for No. 1 and No. 1A
     ESS offices. Those weights sum to 100; customer reports carry ten of
     them, and that is the component scored here.

     The plan scored an office across ten components. You are scored on the
     customer reports component out of 100, because scoring you across the
     whole plan would mean total failure on the one component you work
     could still cost only ten of the office's hundred points. 'qual index'
     shows both numbers.

     handoff relieve signs off the shift, banks the index and starts a new
     board. Work you did not finish carries forward.

     Two clocks run and they are not the same clock. A report's commitment
     runs on elapsed time: the customer is out of service whether or not
     you are doing anything, so the repair force's hours count against it.
     Your own working day runs on your time, because while the field is out
     on one report you are working the next. Shift events come due on your
     working time rather than on the wall clock, which in real time would
     leave every afternoon event unreachable. Eight hours and the wire
     chief calls time.

THE OTHER CRAFT
     You are not alone on the system. Four channels reach you, each
     period-correct:

     write(1)        Terminal to terminal on the same UNIX system.
                     Interrupts you where you sit.
     mail(1)         Reaches you whether you are at the terminal or not.
     order wire      The maintenance circuit between offices, the field and
                     the switching control centre.
     teletype        The switching machine's maintenance TTY, printing on
                     its own schedule. Nobody sends these; the office does.

     who                        Who is on the system
     write <user> <message>     Interrupt somebody's terminal
     mail                       What is waiting for you
     orderwire                  The maintenance circuit

     The people are invented. The jobs, the places and the things they say
     about the plant are drawn from the operations the bundled documents
     describe.

BELL SYSTEM PRACTICES (BSP) REFERENCE
     Standard BSP section numbering:
     
     BSP 000-000 series    General Information and Master Indexes
     BSP 100-000 series    Test Equipment
     BSP 309-000 series    Switched Services Networks
     BSP 460-000 series    Customer Equipment - General Information
     BSP 620-000 series    Outside Plant - General
     BSP 660-000 series    Test Center Operation
     BSP 795-000 series    Common Language
     BSP 800-000 series    Equipment Design Requirements
     BSP 900-000 series    Outside Plant Engineering

     A practice number is nine digits in three groups: the division, then
     a layer and sublayer within it, then the key number of the individual
     practice. Revisions are Issues. There was no division for computing
     systems; UNIX documentation was not a Bell System Practice.
